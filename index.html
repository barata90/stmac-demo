STMAC — live reconstruction console

Training-free solar resource reconstruction via cellular sheaves. Break the Saudi sensor network with realistic block outages, then watch the sheaf solver repair it — right here, in your browser. No GPU, no training data, no learned weights — and the default dataset is 70 days of the real 1999 Saudi NLR record, shipped with this page. The solver here is the one in the paper: a single sparse space-time system on departures from each station's diurnal climatology. Next to it runs GraphTRSS + MDV, the strongest of the recent graph baselines the paper compares against.

New here? How to read this console — 60 seconds
1.The map shows 11 real sensors that measured sunlight across Saudi Arabia through 1999. Lines join neighbouring stations that lend each other information during repair — thicker line, stronger coupling.
2.Break the network deletes data on purpose, imitating how sensors really fail (comms loss, maintenance, calibration drift): in contiguous blocks, not random specks. “Block length” is how long each blackout lasts — the paper’s key stress test.
3.The reconstruction chart: amber is what the sensor truly measured; the shaded band is what we deleted; rose is the conventional repair (each station smoothing its own history); green is the station climatology; teal is STMAC; blue and orange (switch them on in the legend) are STMAC without its climatological prior and the GraphTRSS + MDV baseline. The closer a line hugs amber inside the band, the better the repair.
4.The error cards average the mistake, in W/m², over deleted cells between 08:00 and 16:55 local time only — smaller is better. The fourth card is the station climatology: each station's own mean diurnal cycle, the baseline any spatial method has to beat. The fifth is GraphTRSS + MDV, the strongest of the recent graph methods in Table II, run on the same climatological anomalies; the sixth is STMAC without that prior (S0), the ablation of Table I.
5.The sweep shows how each method behaves as blackouts stretch from 30 minutes to 7 days: smoothing a station's own history explodes, the climatology stays flat, and STMAC tracks the climatology while staying below it. GraphTRSS + MDV leads on short and medium gaps and meets STMAC on multi-day gaps, as in Table II.
6.The Vision 2030 panel turns those errors into money with the paper's unit-commitment simulation of the 2025 Saudi system: how much extra fuel and start-ups each method's error costs a year at 19.3 or 40 GW of PV, with oil valued at its export price. The gap to the climatology is about half a million dollars a year at 40 GW; the gap to the trained Transformer is 1.9 million, and 7.0 million with error-sized reserves at 96 USD/bbl.
7.The badges up top are the thesis: no training set, no fitted model, two scalar weights chosen from observed data, solved inside your browser. The deep-learning baseline needs 136,075 trained parameters and 25 epochs to do the same job.

Vision 2030 · what reconstruction error costs the Saudi power system

Section V-D and Table III of the paper. What the paper reports (Section VI-F, Table III)
  • The model. An hourly 2025 load estimated from public statistics (chart below), four thermal clusters totalling 86 GW (combined-cycle gas 26 GW, open-cycle gas 20 GW, oil-fired steam 33 GW, oil-fired gas turbines 7 GW), a day-ahead schedule solved as a mixed-integer linear program with spinning reserve of 3% of load plus 5% of PV, and real-time redispatch on the actual PV in which combined-cycle and steam commitments stay fixed and gas turbines may start. PV is split equally over the 11 sites (capacity factor 0.21); sites with an outage use the reconstructed irradiance. Oil burned for power is oil not exported, so all fuel is valued at export parity: 69 USD/bbl, the 2025 Brent average (11.9 USD/MMBtu).
  • At 40 GW. No case sheds load; the cost is extra fuel and start-ups when a schedule built on the estimate is corrected in real time. Under the 3+5 rule the error of STMAC costs 2.0 million USD per year, 1.9 million less than the Transformer (0.8 to 3.4 million across block lengths) and 0.5 million less than the climatology, and burns 25.7 thousand barrels of oil equivalent per year against 118 thousand for the Transformer. GraphTRSS + MDV costs 0.6 million less than STMAC, in line with its RMSE; the ranking holds in two further load realisations (1-day blocks).
  • From 19.3 to 40 GW the costs rise 5.7 to 7.4 times, faster than capacity: the error volume roughly doubles and the cost per MWh of error also rises (0.77 to 2.68 USD for STMAC).
  • Reserves and oil price. Reserves sized to each method’s error roughly double the costs. At the EIA 2026 outlook of 96 USD/bbl the margin of STMAC over the Transformer is 4.4 million USD a year under the 3+5 rule and 7.0 million with error-sized reserves (0.7 and 1.1 million over the climatology).
  • Where the cost comes from. Over-estimating PV under-commits slow units, so gas turbines 18 to 22 efficiency points below the combined cycles start in real time; under-estimating leaves slow units near minimum load or displaces PV.
  • First-order model. Pricing the RMSE at imbalance penalties of 3 to 10 USD/MWh gives 7.7 to 25.6 million USD for STMAC at 40 GW, 4 to 13 times the simulated value: per MWh of absolute PV-estimate error the simulated cost has a median of 1.26 USD (0.33 to 3.50 under the 3+5 rule).
  • Validation and limits. With 12.3 GW of renewables and perfect information the model generates 406 TWh of thermal energy (404 TWh implied by Saudi Energy’s report) at 41.7% mean efficiency (38.15% reported) and burns 770 thousand b/d of liquid fuel (991 thousand reported), so it likely understates the fuel cost of errors. It is a single-node fleet with an assumed technology split, PV at the 11 historical sites and a 10% outage fraction, without network constraints, battery storage or financing premia.
Simulation input · estimated 2025 hourly national load (daily range and mean)

No national hourly load is published, so the paper estimates it by hierarchical moment matching: monthly regional mean, maximum and minimum load from ECRA (2015 to 2019) set the seasonal shape and daily amplitude, 2025 regional sales weight the regions, typical daily curves and a 10 to 15% Friday reduction shape each day, and a log-normal AR(1) factor adds weather variation, scaled to the 2025 peak of 77.08 GW and calibrated to 429.1 TWh. The weekday calendar is that of 1999, so each day lines up with the irradiance it is simulated against.

Glossary for non-specialists
·Irradiance (GHI, W/m²) — sunlight power landing on one square metre of horizontal ground. Roughly 0 at night, 900–1000 at midday in this winter window, up to 1100+ in summer.
·Block outage — a sensor stops reporting for a continuous stretch (30 minutes to 7 days), as happens with communication failures or maintenance. Harder than scattered missing points, because whole sunrise-to-sunset arcs vanish.
·RMSE — root-mean-square error: the typical size of the mistake, in W/m², measured only on the deleted cells between 08:00 and 16:55 local time (clock hours 8 to 16, the scoring window of the paper's notebooks). Lower is better.
·Pure temporal — repair a station using only its own history, by smoothing. Excellent for minutes-long gaps, useless for gaps longer than a day.
·Station climatology (MDV) — the station's usual value at that time of day over the surrounding ±15 days. A strong, simple baseline for long gaps; any smarter method must beat it.
·Sheaf, longitude sheaf — a rule for how neighbouring stations are allowed to inform each other. The longitude sheaf first shifts every station into its own solar time, so that noon at one station is compared with noon at its neighbour. The shift is δ = (λ − λ̄)/(15°/h), measured from the network-mean longitude λ̄.
·STMAC (Spatial-Temporal Maximum Accuracy Computing) — the paper's method: take the departure from the climatology, ask that it be smooth in time and consistent with neighbours in solar time, and solve that as one sparse linear system. No training set, no fitted model.
·S0 — STMAC without its prior (C = 0, g = 0, r = 10), the ablation of Table I. The gap between S0 and STMAC is what the station climatology contributes.
·GraphTRSS + MDV, TVGSR, RTVGSR — recent time-varying graph signal recovery methods (2017 to 2025) that the paper compares against in Table II. They penalise how much neighbouring stations disagree in their changes from one time step to the next, using the ordinary graph with no solar-time shift. Applied to the same departures from the climatology (“+ MDV”), GraphTRSS is more accurate than STMAC for gaps up to one day; STMAC is level at 3 days and ahead at 7 days. GraphTRSS + MDV runs live on this page.
·Unit commitment — the daily decision of which power plants to switch on, made a day ahead on a forecast of load and solar output. When the solar estimate is wrong, fast but inefficient gas turbines start in real time; the paper prices each method’s error as that extra fuel and start-up cost, with oil valued at the price it would fetch as an export.
·r and g — the only two knobs, the ratios r = αt/αs and g = γ/αs of Eq. (5). r says how much temporal smoothness counts relative to neighbour agreement; g says how strongly long gaps are pulled back toward the climatology. The paper selects them once: for each outage length it hides 5% of the observed cells in blocks of that length and keeps the pair with the lowest error averaged over the seven lengths.
