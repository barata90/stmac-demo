STMAC — live reconstruction console

Training-free solar resource reconstruction via cellular sheaves. Break the Saudi sensor network with realistic block outages, then watch the sheaf solver repair it — right here, in your browser. No GPU, no training data, no learned weights — and the default dataset is 70 days of the real 1999 Saudi NLR record, shipped with this page. The solver here is the one in the paper: a single sparse space-time system on departures from each station's diurnal climatology.

3.The reconstruction chart: amber is what the sensor truly measured; the shaded band is what we deleted; rose is the conventional repair (each station smoothing its own history); green is the station climatology; teal is STMAC; blue (switch it on in the legend) is STMAC without its climatological prior. The closer teal hugs amber inside the band, the better the repair.
4.The error cards average the mistake, in W/m², over deleted cells between 08:00 and 16:55 local time only — smaller is better. The fourth card is the station climatology: each station's own mean diurnal cycle, the baseline any spatial method has to beat. The fifth is STMAC without that prior (S0), the ablation of Table I.
5.The sweep shows how each method behaves as blackouts stretch from 30 minutes to 7 days: smoothing a station's own history explodes, the climatology stays flat, and STMAC tracks the climatology while staying below it.
6.The Vision 2030 panel turns those errors into money: what forecast-error penalties would cost a 40+ GW solar fleet per year for each method's error level. The gap to the climatology is small; the gap to the trained Transformer is not.
7.The badges up top are the thesis: no training set, no fitted model, two scalar weights chosen from observed data, solved inside your browser. The deep-learning baseline needs 136,000 trained parameters and 25 epochs to do the same job.

What the paper reports (Table II: full year, 20 masks, averaged over the seven block lengths)
  • The pure-temporal bar is a reference, not an operating baseline. Averaged over the seven block lengths its RMSE is 1,209 W/m², and at a 3-day gap alone it reaches 1,904 W/m², beyond the physical range of sunlight; clipping its output to [0, 1400] W/m² brings that to 658.
  • The honest comparisons are the other three bars. Against the trained Transformer, STMAC saves $3.3M to $14.6M per year across the paper's three scenarios (40 GW at $3/MWh, 52.7 GW at $5/MWh, 52.7 GW at $10/MWh). The Transformer is the only method with a training period and the full-year figures include it, which can only lower its error, so this margin is conservative. On the held-out window, where all three methods fall by nearly the same factor (0.59, 0.59, 0.61), the margin is $1.8M to $7.9M over the Transformer and $0.4M to $1.7M over the climatology.
  • Against the station climatology, STMAC saves $0.9M to $3.9M per year, because averaged over the sweep the two differ by 16.8 W/m². Restricted to cloudy cells the difference is 46.5 W/m² and the saving becomes $2.4M to $10.7M, a cloud-dominated stress year rather than a share of the all-cell figure; at 3-day gaps alone it narrows to 2.9 W/m² and $0.2M to $0.7M. Use the source toggle above to move between the three.
  • Replacing the RMSE in Eq. (7) by the mean absolute error, deadband (5%, 10%), tiered or asymmetric settlement, evaluated on the residuals of five masks at the 3-day block, lowers the cost of STMAC by 38% to 73% and keeps it at or below the climatology in all six structures. A tiered rate of twice the base above a 15% deviation raises it by 20%, so Eq. (7) is a first-order estimate, not a bound.
  • Eq. (7) prices one narrow stream, so all of these figures are a lower bound on operational value. Reserve sizing, curtailment avoidance, the financing risk premium on resource-data quality, and the observability of newly commissioned stations that have no history for a learned model are left unpriced.
  • The value grows with network density. On the 2019 satellite field the margin over the climatology, priced at 52.7 GW and $10/MWh, rises from $3.5M to $5.0M per year between 11 and 50 sites, so an added monitoring site is worth about $69k per year at first and about $21k per year near 50 sites. That planning curve is still to be re-measured on K.A.CARE ground data.
Glossary for non-specialists
·Irradiance (GHI, W/m²) — sunlight power landing on one square metre of horizontal ground. Roughly 0 at night, 900–1000 at midday in this winter window, up to 1100+ in summer.
·Block outage — a sensor stops reporting for a continuous stretch (30 minutes to 7 days), as happens with communication failures or maintenance. Harder than scattered missing points, because whole sunrise-to-sunset arcs vanish.
·RMSE — root-mean-square error: the typical size of the mistake, in W/m², measured only on the deleted cells between 08:00 and 16:55 local time (clock hours 8 to 16, the scoring window of the paper's notebooks). Lower is better.
·Pure temporal — repair a station using only its own history, by smoothing. Excellent for minutes-long gaps, useless for gaps longer than a day.
·Station climatology (MDV) — the station's usual value at that time of day over the surrounding ±15 days. A strong, simple baseline for long gaps; any smarter method must beat it.
·Sheaf, longitude sheaf — a rule for how neighbouring stations are allowed to inform each other. The longitude sheaf first shifts every station into its own solar time, so that noon at one station is compared with noon at its neighbour. The shift is δ = (λ − λ̄)/(15°/h), measured from the network-mean longitude λ̄.
·STMAC (Spatial-Temporal Maximum Accuracy Computing) — the paper's method: take the departure from the climatology, ask that it be smooth in time and consistent with neighbours in solar time, and solve that as one sparse linear system. No training set, no fitted model.
·S0 — STMAC without its prior (C = 0, g = 0, r = 10), the ablation of Table I. The gap between S0 and STMAC is what the station climatology contributes.
·r and g — the only two knobs, the ratios r = αt/αs and g = γ/αs of Eq. (5). r says how much temporal smoothness counts relative to neighbour agreement; g says how strongly long gaps are pulled back toward the climatology. The paper selects them once: for each outage length it hides 5% of the observed cells in blocks of that length and keeps the pair with the lowest error averaged over the seven lengths.
